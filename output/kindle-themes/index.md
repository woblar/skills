## Shared style prompt

Create one finished AI raster educational illustration on a pure white background, a hand-drawn pastel whiteboard, with slightly imperfect black marker lines, simple line icons or stick people, restrained powder blue, butter yellow, mint, lavender and blush accents, generous white space, big legible hand-lettered print, and a monochrome screen on every Kindle. Teach a curious nontechnical adult. The Kindle can be recognizable as a simple slim e-reader, no logos. Render only the requested words, excellently spelled. No URLs, sources, page decorations, framing device, branding, logos, or faux manuscript paragraphs. Leave ample margin. Keep the illustration coherent without seeing the other nine. Make the requested composition unmistakable.

## Layout prompts

The initial prompt for each image was followed by the shared style prompt.

### 01 Pipeline

Landscape. The entire graphic is ONE large connected hand-drawn horizontal pipe from a book icon on left to a finished Kindle page on right, with four consecutive colored chambers separated by bands inside the same channel, left-to-right simple arrows. No detached cards, table, or panels. Render title “HOW A KINDLE MAKES A PAGE”. Exact chamber labels with short captions: “DOWNLOAD” / “Save the book on the Kindle.”, “LAY OUT” / “Fit the words to the chosen text size.”, “UPDATE” / “Electric fields move tiny black and white particles.”, “HOLD” / “The display keeps the new page visible.” Under outlet only, render “The screen reflects light like paper.”

### 02 Comparison grid

Landscape. A SINGLE clean hand-drawn ruled matrix with exactly three column headings “PART”, “WHAT IT DOES”, “WHEN IT MATTERS”, plus four horizontal data rows. Modest icons in first-column cells only, no separate panels, arrows or cutaway. Title “WHAT DOES EACH PART OF A KINDLE DO?” The four rows, in order: “STORAGE” | “Keeps the downloaded book” | “Reading offline”; “SOFTWARE” | “Arranges words at your chosen size” | “Opening a book or changing the font”; “E INK DISPLAY” | “Moves pigment to update a page” | “Turning a page”; “FRONT LIGHT” | “Lights the screen from the front” | “Reading in dim light”. Small text underneath “The display holds a still page without continuous power.”

### 03 Four-frame comic

Wide landscape four comic frames in ONE continuous horizontal strip, four equal illustrated moments, natural speech bubbles from a consistent friendly stick-person reader and their Kindle, large readable type, no flowchart cards. Title “A KINDLE PAGE TURN, IN FOUR FRAMES”. Frame 1 at an armchair shows reader tapping the right edge, bubble “Next page, please.” Frame 2 an imaginative mini cutaway showing black and white grains in a little capsule during the update, bubble from reader “What changes inside?” with tiny separate caption “An electric field moves the pigment.” Frame 3 reader sees fresh page in sunlight, bubble “There are the new words!” caption “The screen reflects light.” Frame 4 same reader settled, bubble “Now it can just sit there.” caption “The screen holds the page without continuous power.” Use literal clearly different actions while preserving reader and device. No repeated footer.

### 04 Annotated object

Title “WHAT’S INSIDE A KINDLE?” Make a small physical cutaway corner revealing an abstract chip and battery behind the screen. Five short labels exactly: “TOUCHSCREEN” / “Tap to turn a page.” points to finger at screen edge; “E INK LAYER” / “Pigment moves to update the picture.” points into the screen; “FRONT LIGHT” / “Lights the screen surface.” points to a subtle warm line along front screen edge; “PROCESSOR AND STORAGE” / “Keep the book and lay out the text.” points to chip behind screen; “BATTERY” / “Powers updates, lights and electronics.” points to distinct battery shape behind screen. One optional small circular inset connected to E Ink layer shows white particles at the viewing surface and dark particles at the bottom in one tiny light spot, caption “A light spot, magnified”. No side-by-side screens, box grid or invented engineering dimensions.

### 07 Bold takeaway poster

Three small nearby illustrated callouts: “Power moves the pigment when the page changes.”, “Light reflects off the page.”, “The front light and other electronics still use power.” Visually subordinate callouts. No multi-panel design or footer strip. The main mental model is the display’s ability to keep the visible page, not a claim the device needs no battery.

Preserve the four letters, every heading, every caption, the rest of the icons, palette, and all other aspects of the original artwork.
