# Style and quality

Use a white background, slightly imperfect black marker outlines, large readable handwritten lettering, simple line icons or stick figures, and restrained pastel blue, butter yellow, mint, lavender, and blush. Give the main illustration room to breathe. Aim for roughly 60–150 visible words and remove detail before reducing type size. Show the mechanism through objects, relationships, and actions. Skip presenter overlays, screenshot borders, logos, gradients, and repeated footer blocks.

Check that arrows show the correct direction, labels identify the right parts, and objects and conditions stay consistent across panels unless the change is the lesson. Identify analogies as analogies. Show whether compared concepts are alternatives, complements, or one part of the other. Match product-specific claims to current primary documentation. Keep illustrative numerical displays and deformation clearly conceptual. Use a verified artifact for measurements, calculated results, or construction detail.

Before delivery, check that a learner can explain the main idea in one sentence, the layout is recognizable without the title, and labels are readable and correct. For a batch, check that each image stands alone and the compositions differ. Recheck material corrections before returning the result.
